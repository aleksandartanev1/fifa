Governance & organisation

Inside FIFA

How FIFA is run, what it does for the global game, and the objectives guiding the organisation through 2027.

2023–2027

Strategic Objectives

  • Grow participation in football worldwide, on and off the pitch
  • Enhance the quality and standing of FIFA competitions
  • Strengthen the football ecosystem and its stakeholders
  • Maximise the sustainable growth of the global game
  • Protect the integrity of football competitions
  • Position FIFA as a trusted and transparent organisation
Focus areas

What FIFA does

Legal

Rules and reports, legal portal, Football Tribunal, decisions, anti-doping, compliance, integrity and education programmes.

Transfer system

FIFA Clearing House, agents, transfer reports, transfer windows and education about transfers.

Women's Football

Strategy, club licensing, coach mentorship, professionalisation, benchmarking and health reports.

Advancing football

FIFA Forward, club management, professionalisation, benefits programmes and support for leagues.

Refereeing

International refereeing lists, tournament refereeing and the Laws of the Game.

Innovation

Video assistant referees, goal-line technology, playing surfaces, certified installations and providers.

Talent development

Football metrics, technical reports, coaching resources, talent academies and the FIFA Training Centre.

Tournament organisation

Bidding processes, sustainability, legacy, volunteering, media rights and broadcasters.

Since 1904

History

FIFA was founded in Paris in 1904 by seven national associations to oversee international competition among national associations. Since then, the organisation has grown to include 211 member associations and organises some of the most watched sporting events in the world.

Over the decades, FIFA's role expanded beyond the men's World Cup to include women's football, youth competitions, futsal, beach soccer and the ongoing development of the Laws of the Game.
